The approach

Forward Deployed Engineering, not a slide deck.

Most integration work stalls in the handoff between the people who scope it and the people who build it. GRAPH removes that handoff — the engineer who designs the connection is the one who ships it, inside your tools, on your team's cadence.

01 — Embed

Join the workflow, not a project plan

A defined engagement inside your existing tools and meetings — not a discovery deck followed by a proposal.

02 — Build

Ship into production directly

Automations and integrations are built with the people who'll use them, and go live incrementally, not in one big release.

03 — Operate or hand off

Documented, not dependent

Keep it running under the engagement, or transfer it cleanly to an internal owner who actually understands it.

What gets connected

Three problems, one practice.

Automation

AI-driven process automation

Repetitive process work — approvals, routing, status reporting — mapped to AI agents and scripts that plug into the systems already in place, instead of a new dashboard nobody opens.

Integration

Tool integration

Wiring together what a team already pays for — Asana, internal APIs, spreadsheets, and the rest — into a single flow, so information stops being re-typed between systems by hand.

Infrastructure

Knowledge infrastructure

Notes that link to each other the way the work actually connects, with backlinks and a visual map that surface a related decision the moment it matters again. Everything stays as plain, portable text that people and AI can both read directly, instead of being locked inside a tool only one person knows how to use.

A note on blockchain

Blockchain shows up in this practice only where it earns its place — a smart contract, a system that genuinely needs shared, tamper-evident state. It isn't a menu item, and it isn't featured here for its own sake; it's a tool considered like any other, and used rarely.

Example pipeline

An inbox that files its own tasks

Tap to see how it works ↻

Incoming email is read and summarized automatically, checked against the knowledge vault for context and open threads, and turned into a task on the team's kanban board, landing where the work already gets tracked instead of sitting in an inbox waiting to be triaged by hand.

  1. Email arrives
  2. Summarized
  3. Checked against the knowledge vault
  4. Task created on the board
Example pipeline

A closed project leaves something behind besides a closed ticket

Tap to see how it works ↻

When a project or ticket is marked done, the key decisions and blockers from its notes and threads are pulled together automatically and drafted into a short lessons-learned note. That note is linked into the relevant process note, so the next project starts from what was actually learned instead of a blank page.

  1. Project closed
  2. Notes + threads reviewed
  3. Retro drafted
  4. Linked for next time
Example pipeline

An expense lands in the right place on its own

Tap to see how it works ↻

A receipt or invoice is read automatically, with the amount, vendor, and category pulled out and logged against the right budget note. Anything over threshold is flagged immediately, instead of surfacing weeks later during reconciliation.

  1. Receipt arrives
  2. Details extracted
  3. Logged to budget note
  4. Flagged if over threshold
Flagship capability

Your knowledge base, wired for AI.

Most teams' process knowledge lives scattered across documents, chat threads, and the people who happen to remember. GRAPH's flagship approach turns that knowledge into a knowledge graph, a web of linked notes that mirrors how the work actually connects, and uses it as the control layer for programme and project management.

Plain text, version-controlled, and directly readable by AI, so automation works from an accurate model of how the work actually runs, not a guess reconstructed from a status meeting.

About

One engineer, embedded.

GRAPH is Olaf — a single practice, deliberately. No bench, no account team: the person you talk to during scoping is the person who writes the integration and sits with your team while it beds in.

The work draws on a background in process management, systems integration, and design-led problem solving — applied the same way whether the task is untangling a reporting process or connecting a knowledge vault to an AI agent.

Staying small is a choice, not a starting point waiting to be outgrown. It keeps the distance between "we should automate this" and "it's running" as short as it can be.
